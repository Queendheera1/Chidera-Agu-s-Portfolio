  <h2><span class="num">02</span>Work Sample: Rubric-Based Review</h2>
  <p class="lead">How I review a deliverable against a rubric and write feedback a contributor can act on — the same method I applied evaluating AI outputs for Apple-related projects.</p>

  <div class="card">
    <h3>Reviewed item: customer-support email draft (sample exercise)</h3>
    <table>
      <tr><th>Rubric criterion</th><th>Score</th><th>Issues found</th><th>Severity</th></tr>
      <tr><td>Answers the customer's actual question</td><td>2/5</td><td>Responds to the wrong billing period; question about the March charge is not addressed.</td><td><span class="sev sev-high">High</span></td></tr>
      <tr><td>Accuracy of information</td><td>3/5</td><td>Refund timeline stated as 3–5 days; policy says 7–10 business days.</td><td><span class="sev sev-high">High</span></td></tr>
      <tr><td>Tone &amp; empathy</td><td>4/5</td><td>Opens well but closing line reads as dismissive ("That's all we can do").</td><td><span class="sev sev-med">Med</span></td></tr>
      <tr><td>Formatting &amp; grammar</td><td>4/5</td><td>One run-on sentence in paragraph 2; missing space after "Hi,".</td><td><span class="sev sev-low">Low</span></td></tr>
    </table>
    <div class="quote">
      <strong>Structured feedback written to the contributor:</strong> "Thanks for the quick turnaround. Two blockers
      before this can pass: (1) the customer asked about a March charge — please answer that directly in the first
      paragraph; (2) our refund policy is 7–10 business days, so '3–5 days' must be corrected to avoid a follow-up
      complaint. Once those are fixed, soften the closing line to e.g. 'Happy to help if anything else comes up.'
      Grammar fixes are minor and noted inline."
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2><span class="num">03</span>Work Sample: Reusable QA Checklist</h2>
  <p class="lead">A checklist I use to keep reviews consistent — no item passes just because it was checked last time. Building and refining checklists like this is how I cut recurring back-and-forth in my operations roles.</p>
  <div class="card">
    <table>
      <tr><th>Stage</th><th>Check</th><th>Pass rule</th></tr>
      <tr><td>1. Setup</td><td>Latest rubric/guideline version loaded</td><td>Must match version in the task brief</td></tr>
      <tr><td>2. Completeness</td><td>All required sections/fields present</td><td>Zero missing sections</td></tr>
      <tr><td>3. Accuracy</td><td>Facts, figures, links verified against source</td><td>Zero unverified claims</td></tr>
      <tr><td>4. Consistency</td><td>Terminology &amp; style match the guide</td><td>Zero unexplained deviations</td></tr>
      <tr><td>5. Documentation</td><td>Every error logged with severity + location</td><td>100% of errors logged</td></tr>
      <tr><td>6. Feedback</td><td>Contributor receives specific, actionable notes</td><td>No vague feedback ("improve this")</td></tr>
      <tr><td>7. Metrics</td><td>Error category &amp; count entered in tracker</td><td>Logged same day</td></tr>
    </table>
